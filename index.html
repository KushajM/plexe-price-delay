Why the delay curve has no model behind it three tried, all lost

Held-out mean absolute error on the log price ratio: carrying the price forward 0.0962, a fitted booking curve 0.0967, a delay-only curve 0.0974. Both structures score negative skill.

A quantile model over 7 rolling origins and 3 seeds, cluster-bootstrapped on hotel, beat the shipped lookup at all 7 origins, but by 0.0033 against a pre-registered bar of 0.005 with 4 of 7 intervals clear of zero. It therefore does not ship, and the bar was not moved.

Hotel identity adds nothing on this axis either: held-out pinball loss for delay by hotel was 0.11099 against 0.11090 for delay alone. A property we have never seen is drawn from the same curve as a familiar one, and loses +0.26% for it.

Pricing a property with no history 400 trees, 0.4052 log-MAE

A property with no quote has no anchor, so the price level itself has to be estimated. Scored with every one of a property's rows withheld across 122,331 hotel-currency units, and with every cell and chain table rebuilt from the remaining hotels only, so no table the model reads was ever built with the property in it.

The model beats the cell lookup by 0.078 of log error, about a sixth. It is a real gain and a modest one: 42% of its estimates land within a quarter of the true rate and one in ten is out by more than 87%. Price a property with it, do not quote a property from it.

Every control above earns its place. Holding the other four fixed across 300 random configurations and sweeping one from end to end moves the estimate by, at the median, 147% for chain, 123% for market, 107% for star band, 94% for property type and 11% for facility count. The form is not decoration around a market lookup.

A number this page published and has withdrawn 0.1928 was leakage

This page previously said the cold-start estimator scored 0.1928 log-MAE with a median error of 8.6%. That was wrong, and the honest figure is 0.4052 with a median error of 30.3%.

Every neighbourhood feature was built leave-one-out, as the subject's cell mean with the subject's own price taken out: (sum − own) / (count − 1). That looks safe, and it passes the obvious test, which is that the feature equals what you would have got had the property never existed. It is not safe. Conditional on the cell total the value is a strictly decreasing function of the subject's own price, so a learner that can estimate the total from anything else recovers the price. The feature set handed it two ways to do that.

The test that catches it is to shuffle the target within currency, rebuild every feature against the shuffle, and refit. A clean feature set then scores the currency baseline of 0.6100, because nothing predictable is left. The original set scored 0.3166. Rebuilding so that a property's tables are computed only from other properties puts the shuffled score back at 0.6189 and the real score at 0.4052.

The delay curve above is unaffected: it holds no fitted parameters and no such feature.

How a pair is built method and exclusions

A hotel is only listed when it can answer all three horizons on 12 or more of its own pairs per window, which is why the list is and not more. A tenth percentile over fewer than eleven points is just the minimum.

3,157 hotels in the source carry coordinates of exactly (0,0), putting Baku, San Diego and Bogota in one neighbourhood. They are excluded from every geographic feature rather than treated as a place.

Every number here is computed from real bookings, and nothing is illustrative. The delay curve is not a forecast: it reports what comparable bookings did, and the gate that would license a prediction was not met. The rate shown for a property with no history is a model prediction, and is labelled as one wherever it appears. Built by Plexe for ZenIQ.
